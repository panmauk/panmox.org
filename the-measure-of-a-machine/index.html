What is this?

Data and his brother Lore wake up in Main Engineering of a starship with no power, no engines and no crew. Neither knows how they got there. Each remembers his own life, and those memories don't agree.

They find each other. They argue about who is in charge. They work out what is wrong with the ship and, if they can agree for long enough, fix it.

You can watch them, or walk into the room yourself and talk to them.

What makes it different

They aren't reading a script. Every line is decided in the moment by the character's own mind, from what he remembers, what he sees and what he wants. Run it twice and you get two different stories.

They remember. What you tell them, they keep. What they told you, they won't tell you again as if it were new. Things they notice once fade; things that matter to them stay.

They are checked against what they know. Before either brother says anything, the line is checked against his own memory and against the ship. A line that fails is thrown away and drawn again. The checks catch a lot, not everything, so sometimes a wrong line still gets through.

They live in a real place. The ship has real systems and the consoles really work. If the power is off, the transporter doesn't work, for them or for you.

Talk to them

Walk in and they will notice you. They'll ask who you are and what brought you there. Ask Data about Captain Picard, about his trial, about the Crystalline Entity. Ask Lore what he thinks of his brother. Ask either of them what they have figured out so far. Then ask Data to take you to the bridge.

Who makes it

The Measure of a Machine is a fan project. We make it out of love for Star Trek: The Next Generation and curiosity about what a mind needs to be a mind: memory, wants, a body in a place, and words that come from those rather than from a script.
