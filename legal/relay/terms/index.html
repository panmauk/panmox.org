2. What Relay is

Relay is a desktop app for Windows, Linux and Mac. It is for private messages, group chats, and voice and video calls. It works over AltNet, a peer-to-peer network: the computers of the people using it pass messages to each other. Some of those computers are run by us.

  • Messages and calls are end-to-end encrypted. Only the people in a conversation can read it. We cannot read your messages or listen to your calls.
  • Personal mode is for friends, message requests and groups of up to 32 people.
  • Business mode is for workspaces of up to 50 people. Workspaces have channels, direct messages between coworkers, and calls and meetings of up to 8 people.
  • Relay is free. There are no adverts and no paid plans.

Relay is open-source software. Your right to copy, change and share the software itself comes from its licence, not from these terms. These terms cover your use of the service: the AltNet account, @names, our network nodes, the call relay and moderation.

3. Who can use Relay

  • You must be at least 16 years old. When you create an account, you confirm that you are.
  • If you use Relay for an organisation (for example, you create a workspace for your company), you confirm that you are allowed to do that. Section 8 also applies.
  • You may not use Relay if we have banned you, unless we have lifted the ban.

4. Your account

  • You need a free AltNet account to use Relay. It is the same account used for AltNet Studio. We verify your email address with a code.
  • Keep your password secret. You are responsible for what is done with your account, unless someone else used it without any fault on your part.
  • One account works on one computer. To move to a new computer, write to us.
  • You can choose one @name. It is permanent: you can't change it, sell it or give it away. It stays yours while your account is in good standing. Vulgar, sexual or hateful names are refused automatically. We may remove a name that impersonates someone, infringes someone's rights or is abusive, and we will tell you why.
  • Anyone who knows your @name can send you a message request. Until you accept, they can send you at most 3 messages. If you don't choose an @name, people can only reach you with your long Relay ID.

The full rules are in the Community Guidelines, which are part of these terms. In short, you must not use Relay to:

  • promote or support terrorism, or incite violence or hatred;
  • send spam, run scams or phishing, or spread malware;

6. Your content

What you write and send stays yours. Because it is end-to-end encrypted, we don't see it and we don't need any licence to use it. If you report someone and choose to include messages, you send us copies of those messages so that we can handle the report (see the Privacy Policy).

You are responsible for what you send. Once a message has reached someone, it is on their computer. We cannot take it back or delete it there.

7. Reports, bans and appeals

The details are in the Community Guidelines. The key points:

  • You can report anyone in Personal mode. You can choose to include the last 10 messages of the conversation. If you do, they are sent to us for that report only.
  • When three different people report the same account within 30 days, the account is banned automatically. No person at PANMOX checks the case before the ban. Some reports don't count towards this:
    • reports from accounts that our server first saw less than 72 hours ago;
    • reports from people who have reported more than 5 different accounts in 30 days;
    • reports from people whose reports led to two bans that we later overturned.
  • We may also ban an account ourselves after looking at reports, or because an authority orders it.
  • A ban stops the account from using Relay. Its Relay identity can't send messages or call anyone anywhere on the network, and its @name stops working. Messages already on other people's computers are not deleted.
  • When you are banned, the app tells you the reason and how to appeal. You are not told who reported you.
  • You can appeal every ban by writing to abuse.report@panmox.org within six months. A person will review your appeal, consider what you say and answer within 14 days. If the ban was wrong, we lift it.
  • You can also go to court (see section 13).

8. Business mode and workspaces

  • Anyone can create a workspace. The person who creates it is its owner. If the owner leaves, the first member who joined becomes the owner.
  • The owner invites people by @name. Each person decides whether to join.
  • Inside a workspace, reports go to the owner, not to us. A member can report a coworker to the owner and may include up to 10 recent messages. The owner sees who reported whom, why, and those messages, and decides whether to remove the person. Nothing about a workspace report is sent to PANMOX.
  • Coworkers can't block each other inside a workspace. If you have a problem with the owner, you can leave the workspace. In Personal mode you can still block anyone and report them to us, including someone you work with.
  • If you own a workspace, you must also follow the Workspace Terms, which cover how you handle reports and the personal data in them. If you use a workspace for an employer or another organisation, that organisation is responsible for how it uses Relay at work.

9. The app on your computer

  • Relay can start with your computer and keep running in the background so that messages reach you straight away. The Windows installer offers this with the box already ticked. You can turn it off in the installer, or at any time under Account.
  • Because AltNet is peer-to-peer, your computer helps run the network. It stores encrypted pieces of other people's messages and some public network records, and passes traffic on. It can't read those messages. Relay uses some of your internet connection and disk space for this.
  • Your conversations are stored only on your computer, and they are not encrypted there: anyone who can use your computer account can read them. Protect your computer with a password. If you lose the computer or the app's data folder, you lose your conversations. There is no backup.
  • The Windows installer and the Mac build are not code-signed yet. Download Relay only from panmox.org.

10. Availability and changes to the service

Relay is a free service. We try to keep it working, but we can't promise that it will always be available, that every message will be delivered, or that it is free of errors. We may change features, or stop the service. If we plan to stop the service, we will announce it on panmox.org at least 30 days before, where we can.

11. Ending your use

  • You can stop at any time, and delete your account yourself. In the app (version 1.0.4 or later), go to Account → Delete account. We email you a code, then ask for your password and your @name, then ask whether you are sure. Deleting:
    • removes your AltNet account, which is also your AltNet Studio sign-in;
    • removes what our Relay server holds about you;
    • erases everything Relay stored on your computer.

Your @name is never given to anyone else. Deleting your account does not delete messages other people already have. If you can't use the app to delete your account (for example, because the account is banned), write to abuse.report@panmox.org from your account's email address.

  • We may suspend or end your account if you seriously or repeatedly break these terms, if the law requires it, or if your account is used to harm others. Section 7 applies, and you can appeal.
